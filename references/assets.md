# Built-in assets

Every texture, prop and character the renderer draws, by name: what each one is and how it is best used. How to put them in a scene is in `scene_format.md` (Textures, Sprites); `python scripts/assets.py catalog.png` draws them all on one sheet, and `docs/ASSETS.md` keeps the review notes.

## Textures

Built-ins with emissive texels (glow regardless of lighting): `water_surface` (the sea's surface from below), `window_lit`, `window_lit_plaster`, `window_teal`, `runes`, `stained_glass`, `hull_lit` and `hull_red` (a light strip at eye height), `tech_panel` (readouts), `server` (status lights), `blast_door` (a warning light), `grid_floor` (glowing grid lines), `alien_rock` (glowing veins), `fireplace` (the fire), `star_chart` (the stars), `canopy` (light through the leaves, and sky in the gaps), `temple_door` (something burning inside), `conduit` (status lights on a junction box), `screen_wall` (a wall of monitors), `tunnel_roof` (the view up through an aquarium tunnel's roof: deep water, the bright surface far above, a seam of the acrylic at every tile), `jelly_backdrop` (the glowing blue back wall of a jelly tank), `open_water` and `deep_water` (the open water painted on the back of a big tank so the sea seems to go on, paler toward the surface: `open_water` stacked on `deep_water` on a wall two high), `bar_shelves` (bottles on two shelves, each lit from below, for the wall behind a bar), `capsules` and `capsules_lit` (a capsule hotel's berths, four to a unit, each with a glowing number plate and lamp-lit bedding, a drawn blind, a screen's glow or dark inside; `capsules_lit` has more of them occupied - alternate the two cell by cell), `nukage` (toxic slime in slow green swirls: sink it with `drop` and it lights the room from below). Their dark counterparts are `window_dark`, `window_dark_plaster`.

See-through built-ins (see See-through walls): `bars`, `fence_iron`, `chainlink`, `railing`, `picket`, `portcullis`, `window_broken`, `lattice`, `viewport` (an observation window from just above the floor to just under the top: stack two for a glass wall two storeys tall), `tank_window` (a viewing window into an aquarium tank, in a deep frame of dark steel with rounded corners; stand a `glass_pane` just behind it and stack plain wall above), `green_window` (the doomed mode's green plating with a window cut through it), `roof_comb` (a temple's stone lattice crest), and the Doric colonnade: `colonnade` (one fluted column to a cell), `colonnade_top` (capital, architrave, triglyph frieze and cornice) and `colonnade_broken` (shafts snapped off at different heights). Stack them per storey: `["colonnade", "colonnade", "colonnade_top"]` with `h` 3 is an intact colonnade, `["colonnade", "colonnade_broken"]` a ruined one. Seen square-on a colonnade is convincing; seen along its length the columns are flat and thin, so for a row you look down, use `column` props instead. A PNG with transparent pixels behaves the same way.

The built-ins also include gothic windows in pale stone, see-through between their leads (`tall_window`, ending in `tall_window_top`), castle and dungeon stone (`castle`, `flagstone`, `slime_brick`, `door_wood`), winter ground (`snow`, drifts with their lee sides in blue shadow; `ice`, `snow_rock`; `forest_deep_snow`, the deep wood with snow on its boughs, behind a `forest_snow` fringe), the desert (`sandstone` - beds of red, orange and cream standing out as ledges, streaked with varnish; stack it with bands of `sandstone_pale` for a canyon wall - `slot_rock`, the swept curves of a slot canyon, `desert_wash`, the sand and pebbles of a dry wash, and for a cliff dwelling `pueblo_stone` and `pueblo_door`, a T-shaped doorway), the Moon (`regolith`, grey dust pocked with craters), the sea floor (`seabed`, rippled sand under a net of caustic light; `water_surface`, the surface seen from below, glowing, for the roof of an underwater room; `reef_wall`, the rockwork at the back of a reef tank - live rock, coralline, coral heads, algae and polyps), a 1993 shooter's base (`hex_floor`, brown hexagonal tiles; `green_tech`, green plating in vertical strakes; `green_trim`, the same under a band of brown metal with a row of pale teeth, to stack storey on storey; `tech_ceiling`, ceiling tiles in a grid; `slime_edge`, brown plates stained green, for the `side` of a slime pit), the swamp (`swamp_water`, black water skinned with duckweed that mirrors; sink it with `drop` and give it a `bog` `side`; `bog`, peat and sphagnum; `swamp_deep`, grey trunks in the murk hung with moss, for the walls behind a `swamp` fringe), the wood in autumn (`forest_deep_autumn`, the dark between the trunks hung with turned leaves, for the walls behind a `forest_autumn` fringe, and `leaves_fallen` underfoot: leaves of every autumn colour over dark earth), starships (`hull`, `hull_dark`, `hazard`, `deck`, `hex_panel`, plus the lit ones above), alien and dream worlds (`alien_ground`, `organic` living walls, `sky_wall` painted with sky and clouds, `cloud_floor`, polished `obsidian`), a study's `fireplace` and `star_chart`, and a saloon's walls - `saloon_wainscot` under `saloon_paper`, the back bar `saloon_backbar` under `saloon_backbar_top`, `saloon_window` (open panes, see-through, so the sun comes in) - and its `tin_ceiling`; a glasshouse's `glasshouse` walls (open panes in white cast iron, see-through) and `encaustic` tiles; a mine's `mine_rock`, `mine_rock_vein` and `mine_rock_gold` (the gold glows a little, so a lamp seems to catch it). `python scripts/assets.py catalog.png` draws them all, with see-through textures over a checkerboard.

**Clockwork.** `brass_plate` (riveted brass panels, worn bright at their edges), `copper_pipes` (a wall run with copper pipes, a valve wheel, green where they weep, dark iron behind), `clock_face` (a clock dial on a dark ground, one to a cell: give a tower a storey of it).

**Ice palace.** `ice_block` (big blocks of blue ice, frosted faces paler at their edges, deep blue joints, a crack or two), `frost_wall` (ice grown over with feathery frost), `ice_floor` (polished ice over dark water, cracked white; it reflects).

**Glowing jungle.** All glow: `glow_leaves` (a wall of rainforest at night, its leaves dark blue-green with ribs and tips of light), `lumen_floor` (dark leaf litter with glowing moss and fungus specks), `glow_water` (black water with plankton glowing in swirling streams; it reflects - sink it with `drop`).

**Sky islands.** `meadow` (grass sprinkled with tiny flowers, for an island's top) and `island_rock` (rock laid down in bands of grey and brown with roots working through it, for an island's `side`).

**Salt flat.** `salt_flat` (a dry lake bed of salt: pale plates edged by the raised white rims the salt pushes up as it dries), `salt_water` (that crust under a film of still water, darker, the plates faint below: give it `reflect` 0.9 and a mirror's `reflections`), `salt_brick` (blocks cut from the crust, laid in courses).

**Vector World.** Black ruled in lines of white light, glowing, for a cell's `tint` to colour (`{"base": "vector_grid", "tint": [1.0, 0.3, 0.8]}`): `vector_grid` (a wall of squares, two to a unit each way), `vector_floor` (a line of light at every unit, running away to the horizon - give it `reflect` 0.4), `vector_frame` (a line round the edge of each cell, so a block reads as a box of light; a good `top`), `vector_circuit` (traces at right angles ending in pads and rings), `vector_windows` (a tower's face of window outlines, a few lit), `vector_bands` (bands of light stacked up a wall), `vector_data` (dashes of light streaming down in columns). Under a `vector` sky with a striped sun, with wireframes and a strong light bloom; see `examples/vector_world.py`.

**Jungle, ruins and beach.** These are drawn a newer way (shapes lit from one direction and snapped onto colour ramps; see `scripts/pixelart.py`), so leaves and stones read as themselves even small on screen:

- Jungle: `jungle_leaves` (a wall of rainforest), `canopy` (the leaf ceiling seen from below), `canopy_top` (the canopy seen from above: use it as the `top` of jungle walls), `jungle_floor` (leaf litter and roots), `jungle_bark` (a giant trunk netted with strangler-fig roots), `temple_stone` (big chipped blocks with moss in the joints), `temple_cornice` (the top course of a temple tier: make it the last storey of each tier's wall stack, so a stepped pyramid reads as steps), `glyphs` (carved cartouches), `temple_roots` (blocks with roots poured over them), `temple_door` (a corbelled doorway into the dark), `roof_comb`.
- Ruins: `limestone` (sun-bleached ashlar), `ruin_paving` (worn slabs with grass in the joints), `dry_grass`, and the colonnade above.
- Beach: `beach_sand` (pale, wind-rippled), `wet_sand`, `shallows` (clear turquoise over a caustic-lit bed), `sea` (open water with whitecaps), `bamboo`, `thatch`. `wet_sand`, `shallows` and `sea` reflect by default. Leave the water at ground level on a beach: a `drop` draws a bank along the waterline, which looks like the edge of a pool.
